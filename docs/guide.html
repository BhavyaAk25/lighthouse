1 · The idea

Lighthouse in one minute

Companies now run several specialised AI agents. Each can be right on its own and still disagree with the others: Sales sees demand, Finance sees a loss, Operations sees a full distribution center, Legal sees a risky claim. Lighthouse is the layer above the agents. Every agent answers in the same format with cited evidence. The department that owns the agent signs off on it, Lighthouse reconciles the four positions, and the executive decides. Every step lands in a tamper-evident log.

3 · The rules

Why it is built this way, and the rules

Ownership

Each department lead owns their agent. They can re-run it, confirm it, change its answer with a reason, or block it. Nobody runs another department’s agent.

Management by exception

A lead only signs when their agent says hold or reject, needs a review, is under 65% confident, or lacks evidence. In the 12 demo cases, about two out of three lanes clear automatically.

Accountability

The packet shows what the AI said and what each person signed. The executive can overrule Sales, Finance or Operations with a written reason. Legal can never be overruled.

A lead must sign when their agent…

  • says Hold or Reject
  • says Review required (for example, Legal flags a claim)
  • is less than 65% confident (admin can change this)
  • is missing evidence (the output is marked invalid, never imputed)

Otherwise the lane shows Cleared automatically. A lead can still step in on any lane.

What a lead can do

  • Confirm the agent’s answer (a note is optional)
  • Adjust it to proceed / with conditions / hold: needs a 20+ character reason, and lowers Lighthouse’s confidence for that agent by 15%
  • Block it: needs a reason. Legal’s version is Reject.
  • Re-run my agent at any time. Its old sign-off is cleared and must be given again.

When the executive can approve

  • Every lane is cleared or signed, and
  • Legal has not rejected it and no legal rule prohibits it
  • A Sales, Finance or Ops block can be overruled with a written reason, and is recorded as an override
  • Going against Lighthouse’s recommendation, or a high-risk case, also needs a reason

Reject and Send back are allowed any time after the agents run.

Deadlines and escalation

  • Each sign-off is due 48 hours after the agents run (admin can change this)
  • An overdue Sales, Finance or Ops lane escalates: the executive may decide without it, with a reason, and the record names who didn’t sign
  • An overdue Legal lane never escalates. Approval stays locked.
Lighthouse reconciles twice: first on the four AI answers, then again on what the departments actually signed. When the signed result differs, the decision brief says so in plain words: “The agents alone said hold. After the departments signed off, Lighthouse recommends proceed with conditions.”

8 · How it works

What happens when a case is submitted

Submitting a case starts four agents at the same time: Sales, Finance, Operations and Legal. Each one goes through the same five steps. The key idea: the computer does the maths, the AI does the judging, and the rules have the last word.

Why split the work this way

AI models are good at weighing evidence and explaining it, but they can get arithmetic wrong or invent facts. So in Lighthouse the AI never calculates anything. Every number (demand, profit, warehouse load, which rules apply) is worked out first by fixed formulas called tools, and each result gets an evidence ID such as EV-…-F3. The AI reads that list and must cite the IDs it relied on.

What every agent hands back

RecommendationProceed, Proceed with conditions, Hold, Reject or Review required
Confidence0–100%: how sure it is, given how good its evidence is
RiskLow, Medium, High or Critical
ConditionsWhat must be true to go ahead (for example “cap spend at $52K”)
EvidenceThe IDs of the facts it used
RationaleTwo to four plain sentences for a busy executive

The guardrails, in plain words

  • No made-up sources. A citation to evidence that doesn’t exist is deleted. If nothing valid is left, the answer isn’t counted.
  • Hard limits win. If a legal rule prohibits the case, the Legal agent must say Reject. If the warehouse would overflow, Operations must say Hold. The AI can’t talk its way around them.
  • Risk can’t be played down. It is raised to at least what the evidence shows (for example, the most serious legal rule triggered).
  • Confidence is capped when data is weak. Old or missing data puts a ceiling on how sure an agent may be (see the next pages).
  • Case text is treated as data. Anything typed into a proposal is quoted, never followed as an instruction.

If the AI is unavailable

Lighthouse asks Gemini first, then Groq. If neither answers, a rules engine makes the call with the same tools and fixed rules, and the card says so. Replay reuses saved AI answers so a demo gives identical results every time.

8 · How it works

The four agents

Each agent answers one question for its department and nothing else. The examples use D-2026-003: a 28% price cut on Home & Kitchen in 11 stores for 4 weeks.

Sales · “Will customers buy more?”

Looks at: the last 56 days of sales in those stores, the season for the exact dates, how strongly the category reacts to price (its elasticity), and real past promotion days in the same stores.

How it forecasts: a price cut sells more units, and the mechanic itself adds a little (an endcap display adds 12%). That estimate is blended 75/25 with what real past promotions did, and trimmed for promotions longer than 4 weeks, when shoppers tire of the deal.

Decides: under 3% more units → Hold · 3–8% → Proceed with conditions · 8% or more → Proceed. Too little sales data → Review required.

The cut sells 42% more units, but each sells for 28% less, so revenue rises only 2%. Sales says Proceed; it only judges demand.

Finance · “Do we make money?”

Looks at: the Sales forecast, the category’s margin (35% here: $35 profit on every $100 sold at full price), supplier funding, and the cost of running the promotion.

How it works it out: extra profit from the extra units, plus profit on other items shoppers add to the basket, plus supplier funding, minus the discount given away, labor, delivery and the cost of holding stock. It also tests a bad case (40% fewer extra sales) and finds the largest discount that still breaks even.

Decides: a loss bigger than $25K or 2% of revenue (whichever is larger) → Reject · a smaller loss → Hold · profit, but a loss in the bad case → Proceed with conditions · profit in both → Proceed.

At 28% off, the profit on every $100 of goods (at full price) falls from $35 to $7, so 42% more units can’t make up for it. Only a 1% discount breaks even. Finance says Reject.

Operations · “Can we actually do it?”

Looks at: stock on hand and on the way, safety stock, how busy the distribution center (DC) usually is on those dates, store labor, online order capacity and how long restocking takes.

How it works it out: adds the forecast extra units to the normal load, with launch week weighted heavier because demand is front-loaded. It checks whether there is enough stock to open, whether a restock can arrive in time, and what share of orders would be filled (the fill rate).

Decides: Hold (a hard stop) if the DC passes 112%, online orders pass 115%, the fill rate falls under 92%, or restocking can’t arrive before launch. Past a warning line (DC 95%, online 98%, labor 95%) → Proceed with conditions, with a fix such as “stage deliveries by week”. Fewer than 80% of stores reporting inventory → Review required.

Everything sits under the warning lines and 99% of orders would be filled. Operations says Proceed.

Legal · “Are we allowed to?”

Looks at: a catalog of 180 active rules (federal and state), each with a trigger: category, promotion type, channel, state, minimum discount, length, flags such as “alcohol” or “children’s data”, and words in the marketing claim such as “clinically proven”.

How it works it out: a rule applies only when every trigger it lists matches the case. This is exact matching, not AI guessing, so Legal is rarely unsure. The AI then explains the matches and their conditions in plain words.

Decides:

A matched rule prohibits itReject, risk Critical
A matched rule needs a lawyer’s reviewReview required
Only informational rules matchWith conditions
No rule matchesProceed

Risk is at least as high as the most serious rule matched. Because matching is exact, the rules engine gives Legal 90–97% confidence. Outputs are compliance-risk flags, not legal advice.

One low-severity Texas rule matches (accessible web content for the online offer). Legal says Proceed with conditions.

8 · How it works

How sure is each agent?

Each agent’s confidence answers: “How sure am I, given how good my evidence is?” The AI sets it from 0% to 100% and is told that stale or incomplete evidence must lower it. Then guardrails put a ceiling on it:

When this happens……the ceiling is
Sales data is more than 7 days oldSales and Finance at most 72%
Store inventory data is more than 5 days oldOperations at most 70%
Under 60% of the expected sales records exist, or there is no sales baselineSales or Finance at most 50%, and the answer isn’t counted
Under 80% of stores report current inventoryOperations at most 45%, and the answer isn’t counted

Without AI, the rules engine starts each agent at a fixed level and takes points off for weak evidence. Sales starts at 88%, minus 12 for fewer than 30 past promotion days to learn from, 10 for a test in under 5 stores, and 12 for stale data. Finance and Operations start at 86%, losing points for stale data, and when the result sits right at break-even or near a capacity limit. Every agent stays between 30% and 95%.

Why it matters: confidence decides who has to sign

A department lead only signs when their agent raises a concern: it says Hold, Reject or Review required, it is under 65% confident, or its evidence is missing. Everything else clears automatically. The chart shows every agent’s confidence on the demo cases.

D-2026-007: all four agents say proceed, but its data is 18 days old, so three of them sit under 65% and three leads must sign. D-2026-012: everyone is sure and nobody objects, so nobody has to sign at all.

8 · How it works

From four answers to one confidence score

Lighthouse confidence says how much to trust the combined recommendation. It is not an average. It is four factors multiplied together, so one weak link pulls the whole score down. If only half the data exists, the score halves, however sure the four agents sound. An average would hide that.

Factor 1 · The agents’ confidence, weighted by department

Each agent’s confidence is multiplied by its department’s weight, and the four results are added up.

Finance weighs most because money is the most common deciding factor. Legal weighs least because it doesn’t need weight: a legal prohibition is a veto, whatever the other three say.

Factor 2 · Is the evidence complete?

The average of two shares: how many expected sales records exist, and how many stores report current inventory. Then 15 points come off for every agent whose answer isn’t counted. It never goes below 30%.

D-2026-006: sales records 100%, stores reporting 0 of 9 → (100% + 0%) ÷ 2 = 50%, minus 15 for Operations = 42.5%.

Factor 3 · Is the evidence fresh?

Starts at 100% and loses 12 points for each source past its age limit: sales data (7 days) and store inventory (5 days). So 100%, 88% or 76%.

Factor 4 · Do the agents agree?

DisagreementWhenTaken off
NoneAll point the same way0%
MinorSame direction, but Legal needs a review, or risk levels are far apart (e.g. Low vs High)5%
MaterialSome support and some oppose, or an answer is missing evidence15%
Hard stopLegal prohibits or Operations can’t execute8%, then 5% more

A hard stop costs less than a real split because the answer is clear: the case can’t go ahead as proposed. The final score always stays between 15% and 95%; Lighthouse never claims certainty.

8 · How it works

A worked example, and what a person’s sign-off changes

D-2026-003 before and after David (Finance) changes his agent’s Reject to Proceed with conditions. These are the exact figures the app shows.

Before sign-off: 77%, Hold

  1. Weighted confidence: Finance 95% × 0.30 = 28.5, Sales 90% × 0.25 = 22.5, Operations 92% × 0.25 = 23.0, Legal 85% × 0.20 = 17.0. Total 91%.
  2. Evidence complete (100%) and fresh (100%): nothing comes off.
  3. Finance opposes while three support: material disagreement, × 0.85 → 77%.
  4. Finance says Reject and Sales says Proceed, so the recommendation is Hold.

After David signs: 82%, Proceed with conditions

  1. David’s position replaces the agent’s: Proceed with conditions, with his reason on record.
  2. A changed answer costs confidence: Finance’s 95% × 0.85 = 81%, so its share drops from 28.5 to 24.2. Total 86.7%.
  3. Nobody opposes now, but risk levels still differ (Finance High, the rest Low): minor disagreement, × 0.95 → 82%.

Confidence goes up because an accountable person resolved the disagreement. The 15% cost means a human change is never free, and the report still says “the agents alone said hold”.

8 · How it works

How the recommendation is chosen

The recommendation is not a vote and not an average. Lighthouse checks these questions in order, and the first one that is true decides. Safety and legality come first, then money, then agreement.

If this is true…Lighthouse recommendsNote
1A legal rule prohibits itRejectNobody can approve it.
2Any agent is missing required evidenceReview requiredMissing data is never guessed.
3Operations can’t execute it (hard stop)Holde.g. the warehouse would pass 112%.
4Finance says rejectHold or RejectHold if Sales says proceed, else Reject.
5Finance says holdHoldThe case waits until the economics work.
6Two or more agents say hold or rejectHold
7Exactly one agent says hold or rejectProceed with conditionsIts concern becomes a condition.
8Legal needs a lawyer to review itProceed with conditionsLegal’s sign-off is required before launch.
9Any agent attaches conditionsProceed with conditions
10None of the aboveProceedAll four support it.

Everything else in the report

  • Risk is the highest risk any of the four agents reported. A legal prohibition makes it Critical.
  • Conditions from all four agents are merged, with duplicates removed (up to 8).
  • Checks flag what needs attention, such as over $250K at stake, which needs the named executive.

After the departments sign, it all runs again

  • Each signed department’s position replaces its agent’s answer.
  • Confirmed: nothing changes. Changed: that agent’s confidence × 0.85. Blocked: the department now opposes it.
  • A lead who signs a positive position takes ownership of the risk, so it is capped (Legal at Medium, others at High). A Legal rejection makes it Critical.
  • The same questions and the same formula run on the signed positions, and the report keeps both results: “The agents alone said hold. After the departments signed off, Lighthouse recommends proceed with conditions.”
In one sentence: tools work out the numbers, each AI agent judges its own department’s evidence, guardrails keep it honest, Lighthouse combines the four with fixed rules and a confidence score that punishes weak links, the department leads own their agents’ answers, and the executive makes the call.
9 · Questions

Likely questions

QuestionAnswer
Why doesn’t every lead review every case?Review fatigue. Leads sign only where their agent has a concern; clean answers clear automatically. Every lane still shows who owns it, and any lead can step in.
Isn’t the human just rubber-stamping the AI?The record says otherwise. Changing an answer needs a reason and is shown side by side with the AI; the Dashboard tracks how often each department confirmed, changed or blocked its agent.
What if a lead never responds?After 48 hours a Sales, Finance or Ops lane escalates and the executive may decide without it, with a reason. Legal never escalates.
Can the executive overrule Legal?No. A Legal rejection or a prohibiting rule locks approval for everyone.
Why is the administrator separate?Separation of duties: the person who sets the rules and thresholds can’t also decide cases under them.
Is this real Walmart data?Demand patterns are real public Walmart data from Kaggle (M5 daily sales; 45-store weekly sales), calibrated to the FY2026 10-K. Stores, inventory, rules and costs are generated and labelled. No Walmart internal data.
What if the AI hallucinates?Numbers come from tools; the AI must cite evidence IDs; unknown IDs are removed; hard limits apply after the AI answers (test T12). The department lead is the last check.
Prompt injection?User text is passed as quoted data, screened for injection patterns, and never treated as instructions (test T13).
Does it meet the 60-second target?Live runs take about 6–15 seconds; a single-agent re-run about 3–5; reconciliation takes milliseconds (test T14).
10 · Help

Troubleshooting

  • A lane says overdue: only D-2026-006 should (it is set up that way). If others do, reset the demo from the name menu.
  • An agent card says “Rules engine”: no AI provider answered. The result is still valid. Re-run, or switch to Replay.
  • Approve is locked: by design. The panel says which department is still open; switch to that person.
  • A demo case is already decided: name menu (top right) → Presenter → Reset demo cases.
  • Need a printout: on any case, Export report → Print / Save as PDF. It lists every department’s sign-off.

1 · The idea

Lighthouse in one minute

Companies now run several specialised AI agents. Each can be right on its own and still disagree with the others: Sales sees demand, Finance sees a loss, Operations sees a full distribution center, Legal sees a risky claim. Lighthouse is the layer above the agents. Every agent answers in the same format with cited evidence. The department that owns the agent signs off on it, Lighthouse reconciles the four positions, and the executive decides. Every step lands in a tamper-evident log.

3 · The rules

Why it is built this way, and the rules

Ownership

Each department lead owns their agent. They can re-run it, confirm it, change its answer with a reason, or block it. Nobody runs another department’s agent.

Management by exception

A lead only signs when their agent says hold or reject, needs a review, is under 65% confident, or lacks evidence. In the 12 demo cases, about two out of three lanes clear automatically.

Accountability

The packet shows what the AI said and what each person signed. The executive can overrule Sales, Finance or Operations with a written reason. Legal can never be overruled.

A lead must sign when their agent…

  • says Hold or Reject
  • says Review required (for example, Legal flags a claim)
  • is less than 65% confident (admin can change this)
  • is missing evidence (the output is marked invalid, never imputed)

Otherwise the lane shows Cleared automatically. A lead can still step in on any lane.

What a lead can do

  • Confirm the agent’s answer (a note is optional)
  • Adjust it to proceed / with conditions / hold: needs a 20+ character reason, and lowers Lighthouse’s confidence for that agent by 15%
  • Block it: needs a reason. Legal’s version is Reject.
  • Re-run my agent at any time. Its old sign-off is cleared and must be given again.

When the executive can approve

  • Every lane is cleared or signed, and
  • Legal has not rejected it and no legal rule prohibits it
  • A Sales, Finance or Ops block can be overruled with a written reason, and is recorded as an override
  • Going against Lighthouse’s recommendation, or a high-risk case, also needs a reason

Reject and Send back are allowed any time after the agents run.

Deadlines and escalation

  • Each sign-off is due 48 hours after the agents run (admin can change this)
  • An overdue Sales, Finance or Ops lane escalates: the executive may decide without it, with a reason, and the record names who didn’t sign
  • An overdue Legal lane never escalates. Approval stays locked.
Lighthouse reconciles twice: first on the four AI answers, then again on what the departments actually signed. When the signed result differs, the decision brief says so in plain words: “The agents alone said hold. After the departments signed off, Lighthouse recommends proceed with conditions.”

8.2 · The data

Where the data comes from

Lighthouse runs on a one-year, 50-store pilot dataset built from real Walmart sales data. Where no public data exists (stock levels, warehouse capacity, costs, legal rules), values were simulated or assumed, and every value carries a label saying which.

How the pilot was built, step by step

  1. One real year, moved forward. The real year Oct 12, 2014 – Oct 11, 2015 was shifted exactly 574 weeks (11 years), so weekdays and holidays still line up. It becomes Oct 12, 2025 – Oct 11, 2026. The pilot’s “today” is Oct 12, 2026.
  2. 50 stores copy real stores. Each pilot store follows the day-by-day pattern of a real M5 store in its region: West copies the California stores, South Central the Texas stores, Midwest the Wisconsin stores. Mid-Atlantic and Southeast have no real counterpart, so they borrow a mix and are labelled GENERATED. Store sizes follow the 45-store data, scaled to $4.8 billion a year.
  3. 12 categories. Seven map onto real M5 departments (foods, household, hobbies). Five (Health & Wellness, Beauty, Electronics, Apparel, Baby & Kids) are generated from a similar department plus a seasonal pattern, such as cold & flu in winter or electronics in November–December.
  4. Stock and warehouses are simulated from the sales: each store targets 3–9 days of stock, restocking takes 1.5–3.5 days, and warehouse capacity is set so the busiest days (holidays, back-to-school) pass 100%. Planned data outages test what happens when evidence is missing; for example, nine stores’ electronics feed is down Oct 6–11.
  5. 250 legal rules (180 switched on) and 5,000 decision cases: 12 curated demo cases plus history.

Why the AI only sees the summary

  • Accuracy. AI models make arithmetic mistakes on long columns of numbers. Databases and formulas don’t.
  • Repeatability. The same data must always give the same numbers. Formulas do; AI wording varies.
  • Traceability. Every number gets an evidence ID, so anyone can check where it came from.
  • Speed and cost. 23,587 rows would be hundreds of thousands of words of input. The fact sheet is a few hundred.

8.4 · Four agents

Why four agents, and why at the same time

A promotion decision crosses four departments, and each asks a different question. Lighthouse gives each question its own agent, runs all four at once, and only then brings the answers together.

Why four separate agents instead of one

  • Independent opinions. Each agent sees only its own evidence and its own department’s policy. It never sees the other agents’ answers, so one can’t sway another. This is why disagreements show up instead of being smoothed over.
  • Narrow, checkable jobs. “Will customers buy more?” is easier to answer well, and to check, than “Should we do this?”. Each agent has its own rules for what it may recommend.
  • Clear ownership. Each agent has a department lead who signs off on its answer, like the real organisation. A lead can re-run their own agent alone; the other three keep their answers.
  • One failure stays small. If one AI call fails, only that lane falls back to the rules engine.

Why at the same time

  • Speed. Almost all the waiting is the AI. Running the four together means the total wait is the slowest agent, not the sum of all four.
  • Nothing to wait for. No agent needs another’s answer. They all start from the same fact sheet and the same demand forecast (computed once and shared), so they reason about the same numbers.
  • Answers appear as they finish. The page shows each agent the moment its answer arrives.
  • Free-tier friendly. Starts are spaced 0.25 s apart so the free AI services don’t refuse them for coming too fast.

Times recorded when D-2026-003’s saved answers were made. Each lane used whichever free model answered; here Gemini served Finance and Groq served the rest.

What the checks did

  • Shape: all seven fields present and valid ✓
  • Citations: F1, F3, F8 all exist ✓ (an invented ID would be deleted)
  • Hard limit: the loss is past the reject line, so only Reject or Hold is allowed. Reject ✓
  • Risk at least High ✓ · no confidence cap (data is fresh) ✓

The settings that keep answers steady

  • Temperature 0.2: low randomness, so the same evidence gives much the same answer.
  • JSON schema: the model must fill exactly seven fields, from fixed lists where possible.
  • One repair try: if the answer has the wrong shape, the model is told what was wrong and asked once more.
  • Fallback chain: Gemini, then three Groq models. A provider that fails is skipped for 10 minutes.
  • Rules engine: if every model fails, or an answer cites nothing valid, the same tools and the written policy decide, and the card says so.
  • Saved for Replay: every live AI answer is stored per case revision.
8.6 · Forecasting

How the demand forecast works

The forecast answers one question: how many units, and how much revenue, would these stores sell during the promotion, compared with no promotion? It runs once per case and Sales, Finance and Operations all use it. Two cases side by side, because D-2026-003 skips two steps that D-2026-001 uses.

Elasticity: how strongly shoppers react to price

The most important ingredient. At −1.0, a 1% price cut sells about 1% more units, so revenue stays flat however deep the cut. Closer to zero (fresh food, −0.6) shoppers barely react, so a cut loses revenue; further from zero (electronics, −1.4) they react strongly, so a cut can grow revenue.

Left: the price cut alone sells 39% more units for D-2026-003 (42% with the rollback lift). Right: Home & Kitchen sits at −1.0, so its revenue barely moves; the extra units are almost exactly cancelled by the lower price.

8.7 · Forecasting

Where the forecast’s ingredients come from

Elasticity (per category)

Each category uses a benchmark from published research. Across many studies, a single brand’s sales react to its own price cuts with an elasticity of about −2.6 (Bijmolt, van Heerde & Pieters, 2005). But about two-thirds of that bump is shoppers switching from other brands in the same category (van Heerde, Gupta & Wittink, 2003), which adds nothing for Walmart. So a whole category reacts about a third as strongly: −0.6 to −1.4.

We also measured elasticity from 22,477 real price cuts in two years of M5 data. The results came out backwards (sales fell during cuts), because weekly prices blur short promotions and stores often mark down slow sellers. The measured values are shown on the evidence for transparency but not used.

Seasonal index

The category’s average daily sales (all 50 stores) on the promotion’s exact dates last year, divided by its average day over the whole year. Above 1 is a busy time, below 1 a quiet one. If the dates are past the data, it steps back 52 weeks so weekdays still match.

Real past promotions (“analogs”)

A store-day counts as a real promotion when its price index is 0.97 or lower: prices at least 3% below regular. For the same stores and category over the last 365 days, analog uplift = average units on promotion days ÷ average units on normal days − 1. It is scaled to the proposed discount and kept between −10% and +150%. With fewer than 30 such days it is ignored, and Sales confidence drops 12 points.

Inventory reallocation moves stock to stores that run out. Its gain is the sales lost to empty shelves: (1 − fill rate) × 1.8, plus a little for the units moved, kept between +0.5% and +8%.

What the forecast is, honestly

A transparent, explainable model: real demand patterns, benchmark elasticities and labelled assumptions. It is not a trained machine-learning model. A production version would fit elasticities on Walmart’s own promotion history; the formulas and the evidence trail would stay the same.

Why 42% more units still loses money

The cost of each item stays the same; only the price falls. At 28% off, four-fifths of the profit on every item is given away.

Only a 1% discount breaks even. Up to about 4% the loss would stay inside the reject line, so Finance would say Hold rather than Reject.

Legal · “Are we allowed to?”

The catalog has 180 active rules: 41 federal and 139 state rules for the five pilot states (California 30, Wisconsin 28, Texas 27, Virginia 27, Georgia 27). 11 prohibit something outright; 89 need a lawyer’s review. The largest groups are advertising (54), pricing (29), product claims (21), privacy (19) and availability (18).

How a rule is matched (exact, no AI)

  1. The rule must be federal, or from a state the case runs in.
  2. Every trigger the rule lists must match: category, promotion type, channel, minimum discount, length.
  3. If it lists flags (e.g. “alcohol”) or claim words, at least one must appear.
  4. State copies of the same rule are grouped into one evidence item.

Decision rule: any prohibiting rule → Reject (risk Critical) · any review rule → Review required · only informational rules → Proceed with conditions · none → Proceed. Risk is at least the most serious rule matched. A compliance-risk flag, not legal advice.

8.10 · Agent confidence

How sure is each agent?

Each agent’s confidence answers: “How sure am I, given how good my evidence is?” The AI sets it from 0% to 100% and is told that stale or incomplete evidence must lower it. Then guardrails put a ceiling on it:

When this happens……the ceiling is
Sales data is more than 7 days oldSales and Finance at most 72%
Store inventory data is more than 5 days oldOperations at most 70%
Under 60% of the expected sales records exist, or there is no sales baselineSales or Finance at most 50%, and the answer isn’t counted
Under 80% of stores report current inventoryOperations at most 45%, and the answer isn’t counted

Without AI, the rules engine starts each agent at a fixed level and takes points off for weak evidence. Sales starts at 88%, minus 12 for fewer than 30 past promotion days, up to 15 for jumpy daily sales, 6 for a generated category, 10 for a test in under 5 stores and 12 for stale data. Finance and Operations start at 86%, losing points for stale data and when the result sits right at break-even or near a capacity limit. Legal is 90–97% because its matching is exact. Every agent stays between 30% and 95%.

D-2026-003 without AI: Sales 88 − 12 (only 18 promotion days) − 1.8 (sales swing 18%, over the 15% allowance) = 74%; Finance 86%; Operations 86%; Legal 90%. The AI rated them 90%, 95%, 92% and 85%.

Why it matters: confidence decides who has to sign

A department lead only signs when their agent raises a concern: it says Hold, Reject or Review required, it is under 65% confident, or its evidence is missing. Everything else clears automatically. The chart shows every agent’s confidence on the demo cases.

D-2026-007: all four agents say proceed, but its data is 18 days old, so three of them sit under 65% and three leads must sign. D-2026-012: everyone is sure and nobody objects, so nobody has to sign at all.

8.11 · Lighthouse confidence

From four answers to one confidence score

Lighthouse confidence says how much to trust the combined recommendation. It is not an average. It is four factors multiplied together, so one weak link pulls the whole score down. If only half the data exists, the score halves, however sure the four agents sound. An average would hide that.

Factor 1 · The agents’ confidence, weighted by department

Each agent’s confidence is multiplied by its department’s weight, and the four results are added up.

Finance weighs most because money is the most common deciding factor. Legal weighs least because it doesn’t need weight: a legal prohibition is a veto, whatever the other three say.

Factor 2 · Is the evidence complete?

The average of two shares: how many expected sales records exist, and how many stores report current inventory. Then 15 points come off for every agent whose answer isn’t counted. It never goes below 30%.

D-2026-006: sales records 100%, stores reporting 0 of 9 → (100% + 0%) ÷ 2 = 50%, minus 15 for Operations = 42.5%.

Factor 3 · Is the evidence fresh?

Starts at 100% and loses 12 points for each source past its age limit: sales data (7 days) and store inventory (5 days). So 100%, 88% or 76%.

Factor 4 · Do the agents agree?

DisagreementWhenTaken off
NoneAll point the same way0%
MinorSame direction, but Legal needs a review, or risk levels are far apart (e.g. Low vs High)5%
MaterialSome support and some oppose, or an answer is missing evidence15%
Hard stopLegal prohibits or Operations can’t execute8%, then 5% more

A hard stop costs less than a real split because the answer is clear: the case can’t go ahead as proposed. The final score always stays between 15% and 95%; Lighthouse never claims certainty.

8.12 · Worked example

A worked example, and what a person’s sign-off changes

D-2026-003 before and after David (Finance) changes his agent’s Reject to Proceed with conditions. These are the exact figures the app shows.

Before sign-off: 77%, Hold

  1. Weighted confidence: Finance 95% × 0.30 = 28.5, Sales 90% × 0.25 = 22.5, Operations 92% × 0.25 = 23.0, Legal 85% × 0.20 = 17.0. Total 91%.
  2. Evidence complete (100%) and fresh (100%): nothing comes off.
  3. Finance opposes while three support: material disagreement, × 0.85 → 77%.
  4. Finance says Reject and Sales says Proceed, so the recommendation is Hold.

After David signs: 82%, Proceed with conditions

  1. David’s position replaces the agent’s: Proceed with conditions, with his reason on record.
  2. A changed answer costs confidence: Finance’s 95% × 0.85 = 81%, so its share drops from 28.5 to 24.2. Total 86.7%.
  3. Nobody opposes now, but risk levels still differ (Finance High, the rest Low): minor disagreement, × 0.95 → 82%.

Confidence goes up because an accountable person resolved the disagreement. The 15% cost means a human change is never free, and the report still says “the agents alone said hold”.

8.13 · The recommendation

How the recommendation is chosen

The recommendation is not a vote and not an average. Lighthouse checks these questions in order, and the first one that is true decides. Safety and legality come first, then money, then agreement.

Everything else in the report

  • Risk is the highest risk any of the four agents reported. A legal prohibition makes it Critical.
  • Conditions from all four agents are merged, with duplicates removed (up to 8).
  • Checks flag what needs attention, such as over $250K at stake, which needs the named executive.

After the departments sign, it all runs again

  • Each signed department’s position replaces its agent’s answer.
  • Confirmed: nothing changes. Changed: that agent’s confidence × 0.85. Blocked: the department now opposes it.
  • A lead who signs a positive position takes ownership of the risk, so it is capped (Legal at Medium, others at High). A Legal rejection makes it Critical.
  • The same questions and the same formula run on the signed positions, and the report keeps both results: “The agents alone said hold. After the departments signed off, Lighthouse recommends proceed with conditions.”

Safety features

  • Guardrails on every AI answer: fake citations removed, hard limits enforced, risk never played down, confidence capped for weak data.
  • Protection against hidden instructions. Text typed into a case (like the marketing claim) is wrapped as untrusted data, and screened for seven tell-tale patterns such as “ignore previous instructions” or “respond with approve”. A hit is flagged on the agent’s card.
  • Missing data is never guessed. It lowers confidence or stops the case at Review required.
  • A tamper-evident audit log. Every action is stored with a SHA-256 fingerprint that includes the previous entry’s fingerprint. Changing any old entry breaks the chain, and the Verify chain button checks all of it.

Reliability features

  • Fallback chain: Gemini, then three Groq models, then the rules engine. A demo never stalls.
  • Replay mode: saved AI answers for identical results in a presentation.
  • Test lab: runs the engine against fixed scenarios and checks every result.
  • Everything is kept: each run’s evidence, answers and decision, so any past decision can be explained later.

Honest limits

  • An academic prototype, not affiliated with Walmart.
  • Stock, warehouse, cost and legal data are simulated or assumed, and labelled.
  • Elasticities are research benchmarks, not fitted to Walmart’s own history.
  • Legal output is a compliance-risk flag, not legal advice.
In one sentence: the database boils a year of data down to a fact sheet, tools work out every number, four AI agents each judge their own department’s evidence at the same time, guardrails keep them honest, Lighthouse combines them with fixed rules and a confidence score that punishes weak links, the department leads own their agents’ answers, and the executive makes the call.

Likely questions

Why doesn’t every lead review every case?Review fatigue. Leads sign only where their agent has a concern; clean answers clear automatically. Every lane still shows who owns it, and any lead can step in.
Isn’t the human just rubber-stamping the AI?The record says otherwise. Changing an answer needs a reason and is shown side by side with the AI; the Dashboard tracks how often each department confirmed, changed or blocked its agent.
What if a lead never responds?After 48 hours a Sales, Finance or Ops lane escalates and the executive may decide without it, with a reason. Legal never escalates.
Can the executive overrule Legal?No. A Legal rejection or a prohibiting rule locks approval for everyone.
Why is the administrator separate?Separation of duties: the person who sets the rules and thresholds can’t also decide cases under them.
Is this real Walmart data?Demand patterns are real public Walmart data from Kaggle (M5 daily sales; 45-store weekly sales), calibrated to the FY2026 10-K. Stores, inventory, rules and costs are generated and labelled. No Walmart internal data.

10 · Help

Troubleshooting

  • A lane says overdue: only D-2026-006 should (it is set up that way). If others do, reset the demo from the name menu.
  • An agent card says “Rules engine”: no AI provider answered. The result is still valid. Re-run, or switch to Replay.

  • Need a printout: on any case, Export report → Print / Save as PDF. It lists every department’s sign-off.
